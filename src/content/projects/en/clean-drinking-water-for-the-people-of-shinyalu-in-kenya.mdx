### The situation on the ground

**Fizi** Territory in the east of the Democratic Republic of the Congo lies on the shores of Lake Tanganyika – one of the largest freshwater lakes in the world. Despite being so close to water, the rural population has very limited access to clean drinking water. Most people live from farming, fishing and small-scale trade and depend on wells and rivers whose water is often contaminated by animal and human waste as well as by pesticides and fertilisers from agriculture.

The consequences are severe: cholera, typhoid and diarrhoeal diseases occur regularly in the region, and child mortality from waterborne infections is particularly high. On top of this come recurring armed conflicts, a lack of public investment in water infrastructure and poor roads that effectively cut many villages off from aid.

In the villages around **Ake**, our local partner organisation **À Portée de Mains (APM)** runs a primary school and a health post. Teachers there have long observed that children regularly miss school because they suffer from waterborne diseases – a direct obstacle to their education and development. This is exactly where our project comes in.

### Project implementation – Phase 1

In May 2025, together with APM, we brought **two AQQAcube water filters** to the community’s key facilities: one to the À Portée de Mains primary school and one to the affiliated health post. Each filter can provide up to 1,200 litres of clean drinking water per day.

The filters’ journey from Berlin to Ake was long and logistically demanding. After being shipped from Germany, they first arrived at the international airport in Bujumbura, Burundi’s largest city, where customs formalities were completed and the filters were stored temporarily. From there they travelled by road across the border to **Uvira** in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Because years of neglect and conflict have made the road from Uvira to Fizi impassable, the filters covered the final stretch by motorised pirogue across Lake Tanganyika to Swima – and from there by bicycle to the installation site in Ake.

<Figure src="wp/2026/05/rdc-01-ake-1.jpg" alt="The filters’ arduous journey across Lake Tanganyika">The filters’ arduous journey across Lake Tanganyika</Figure>

Before the actual installation, the APM team held **awareness-raising sessions** with teachers, pupils, the parents’ committee and the health post staff, in the local languages and with practical demonstrations on the filter. The sessions covered how to operate and care for the filter correctly, as well as basic hygiene rules for handling drinking water.

### Project implementation – Phase 2

Once the filters were installed, a new challenge soon became apparent: the filters do provide clean drinking water, but they first have to be filled with untreated water. Until then, pupils and teachers had to fetch this water over long distances from the Ngovi River. Valuable lesson time was lost, and especially in the dry season there was often not enough water.

<Figure src="wp/2026/05/WhatsApp-Image-2024-12-19-at-17.57.56.jpg" alt="Clean Drinking Water for a School and Clinic in Ake, Democratic Republic of the Congo (photo 4)" />

That is why, in a second project phase in May 2026, we worked with APM to build a **rainwater harvesting system** at the À Portée de Mains school. Metal gutters on the school roof and PVC pipes channel the rainwater into a **5,000-litre tank**. The school’s AQQAcube filter is filled directly from this tank. The stored water also covers other needs at the school, such as toilets and cleaning. The children no longer have to walk long distances to fetch water, and the supply remains stable even in dry weeks.

<Figure src="wp/2026/11/f0c3679d-04df-4054-b427-b4f21e930e83.jpg" alt="Clean Drinking Water for a School and Clinic in Ake, Democratic Republic of the Congo (photo 5)" />

Before construction began, a meeting was held with the village community. The APM team explained the benefits of using rainwater and encouraged the community to take part in the construction and to look after the system afterwards. A technical team from Uvira then built the system together with people from the village, using a mobile welding machine for the gutters, among other things. It was not easy: transporting the materials was difficult, the internet went down at times, and fuel for the welding machine was hard to come by. As a result, the work took longer than planned. Finally, the entire system was tested with water; it is now in operation and working perfectly.

<Figure src="wp/2026/11/e25b4813-311a-4c9c-8a5a-06a9824093e8.jpg" alt="Welding work on the new rainwater tank">Welding work on the new rainwater tank</Figure>

<Figure src="wp/2026/11/f9490ec8-2451-4207-b0c2-6cf1ea2547ad.jpg" alt="Clean Drinking Water for a School and Clinic in Ake, Democratic Republic of the Congo (photo 6)" />

The school management and the villagers were very pleased with the result and hope that other facilities in Fizi Territory will also receive systems like this.

## Sustainability

The sustainability of our work in this project is ensured by three measures:

1. Training (handling the filters and hygiene)
2. Protecting the filters
3. Long-term project monitoring

Teachers, pupils, parents and the health post staff were trained on site in how to use the filters and familiarised with the technology. They also received an introduction to water hygiene so that they can use the filters effectively in the long term.

The filters are kept protected indoors: one in the school office and one at the health post.

<Figure src="wp/2026/11/888a6cf2-d0a9-4c88-932b-a2a13f1e3d45.jpg" alt="Clean Drinking Water for a School and Clinic in Ake, Democratic Republic of the Congo (photo 7)" />

A **local management committee** made up of teachers and parents was set up to ensure long-term operation. It makes sure the filters stay in working order, organises refresher sessions for the pupils every quarter and ensures that everyone has equal access to the clean water.

The rainwater harvesting system is also meant to stay in good condition for the long term: the community and APM take care of it together. The community is to clean the gutters regularly, check the pipes and connections, and service the tank every three months.
